## Question
Does the symbol retain its lived-world identity when material wear is reduced strongly while silhouette, proportions, center, asymmetry and composition remain as stable as possible?

## Hypotheses
H1 FORM-HEART:
The silhouette is the real carrier of identity. Cleaner materiality preserves nearly all the strength.

H2 MATERIAL-JOINT:
The material wear is structurally important. Cleaning it removes human memory and turns the mark back toward designed branding.

H3 INTERACTION:
Neither alone is sufficient; the strongest result needs the current silhouette plus a restrained amount of material evidence.

No winner before A/B human read.

## Baseline
Use Bullet 01 as A.
Do NOT redesign.

Preserve as closely as possible:
- exact four-point grammar;
- dominant vertical axis;
- overall proportions;
- center geometry/density;
- lateral micro-irregularities;
- centered composition;
- monochrome;
- empty neutral field.

## Single mutation
Reduce visible wear/distress/material erosion by approximately 60–70%.

Do NOT:
- perfect the geometry;
- smooth away the functional asymmetry;
- change point lengths;
- redesign the center;
- add color;
- add text;
- add mockup;
- add new texture language.

2. center redesigns itself;
3. asymmetry disappears because the model “beautifies” it;
4. cleaning becomes glossy/vector-corporate;
5. new decorative features appear;
6. background/mockup starts doing identity work;
7. comparison cannot isolate materiality.

## Expected output
One near-identical monochrome sibling of Bullet 01 with substantially reduced surface wear.
